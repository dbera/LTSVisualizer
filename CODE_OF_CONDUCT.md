# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

LTSVisualizer is committed to maintaining a welcoming, professional, and constructive environment for everyone who participates in the project.

This Code of Conduct applies to all project spaces, including GitHub issues, discussions, pull requests, code reviews, documentation, release coordination, and other communication conducted on behalf of the project.

## Expected behavior

Project participants are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.
- Discuss ideas, implementations, and technical decisions rather than attacking individuals.
- Give and receive constructive feedback in good faith.
- Be patient with contributors who have different levels of experience.
- Ask for clarification before assuming negative intent.
- Keep discussions focused on the project and the issue being addressed.
- Respect privacy, confidentiality, intellectual property, and licensing obligations.
- Credit the work and ideas of others appropriately.
- Accept reasonable maintainer decisions, even when opinions differ.
- Help create an environment in which people can participate without intimidation or hostility.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or personal attacks.
- Discriminatory, demeaning, or insulting language.
- Repeatedly disrupting technical discussions or project workflows.
- Publishing private, confidential, or personally identifying information without permission.
- Deliberately submitting malicious code, misleading reports, or unsafe instructions.
- Abusing issue trackers, pull requests, reviews, or discussions for unrelated disputes.
- Continuing unwanted communication after being asked to stop.
- Retaliating against someone who reports a concern in good faith.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional engineering environment.

## Technical disagreement

Technical disagreement is normal and can improve the project when handled constructively.

When participants disagree:

1. Clearly describe the technical concern.
2. Provide evidence, examples, tests, or measurements when possible.
3. Distinguish requirements from preferences.
4. Consider maintainability, compatibility, usability, security, and performance.
5. Avoid repeating resolved arguments without new information.
6. Allow maintainers to make the final decision needed to move the project forward.

A rejected proposal is not a rejection of the contributor. Maintainers may decline changes because of project scope, maintenance cost, compatibility, complexity, timing, or limited resources.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies when an individual:

- Participates in the LTSVisualizer repository or related project spaces.
- Represents the project in public or private communication.
- Uses an official project account or communication channel.
- Communicates with another participant about project-related work.

Conduct outside project spaces may be considered when it creates a credible risk to the safety, privacy, or ability of others to participate in the project.

## Reporting a concern

Concerns should be reported privately to the repository maintainer rather than through a public issue.

Repository:

<https://github.com/dbera/LTSVisualizer>

Use an appropriate private contact method available through the repository owner's GitHub profile. Include:

- A description of what occurred.
- Where and when the behavior occurred.
- Relevant links, screenshots, or other records.
- Any previous attempts to resolve the matter.
- Whether immediate action may be necessary.

Do not knowingly submit false, misleading, or retaliatory reports.

Security vulnerabilities should be reported according to [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), not through the Code of Conduct process.

## Confidentiality

Reports will be handled as discreetly as reasonably possible. Information may be shared with people who need it to review the concern, protect participants, or take appropriate action.

Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed, particularly when disclosure is required to investigate the report, comply with applicable obligations, or prevent harm.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct. Maintainers may take actions including:

1. Providing a private clarification or informal warning.
2. Requesting an edit, retraction, or apology.
3. Removing comments, issues, pull requests, or other contributions.
4. Restricting participation in a discussion or project area.
5. Temporarily suspending participation.
6. Permanently banning participation in project spaces.
7. Reporting serious conduct to the relevant platform or authority when necessary.

Enforcement decisions will consider the nature, severity, context, impact, and history of the conduct. Actions may be taken without following every step in sequence when the situation warrants a stronger response.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are expected to:

- Apply this policy consistently and in good faith.
- Avoid conflicts of interest where practical.
- Focus enforcement on conduct and impact.
- Document significant enforcement decisions privately.
- Correct decisions when material new information becomes available.
- Avoid using enforcement to suppress respectful technical criticism.

## Good-faith participation

This Code of Conduct is intended to support collaboration, not to punish minor mistakes. Participants are encouraged to acknowledge misunderstandings, correct inappropriate behavior, and continue contributing constructively.

Questions about this policy may be raised through a focused GitHub issue provided the issue does not disclose confidential information about a specific report or participant.
